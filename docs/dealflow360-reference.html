Order Lifecycle

A quotation is one object that carries a status through every stage below. Deal Health (Screen 14) and Reporting (Screen 15) watch it the whole way; the Product Catalog and Discount Tiers (Screens 16–18) are the master data every stage reads from.

State machine

    PendingApproval --> Approved: Approve

    ReturnedForRevision --> Draft: Rep edits quote
    Approved --> Negotiation: Customer requests changes

    Negotiation --> Confirmed: Customer confirms

    Invoiced --> Paid: Payment recorded

    Paid --> [*]
    Confirmed --> SubscriptionActive: Recurring lines detected
    SubscriptionActive --> SubscriptionPaused: Manager pauses
    SubscriptionActive --> SubscriptionCancelled: Cancel
    SubscriptionPaused --> SubscriptionActive: Resume

Stage by stage

1 · Quote creation

Rep opens the Sales Dashboard, starts a quotation, and adds lines. Every discount is validated live against the tier/category ceilings from Discount Config — not only at submit. Upsell suggestions pull from the Product Catalog.

Save Draft keeps status draft. Submit for Approval moves to pending_approval only if a line or blended discount exceeds its ceiling — otherwise the quote can jump straight to confirmed.

2 · Approval routing

Flagged quotes list in Approvals with blended risk and current stage. Approval Detail shows exactly which line broke which ceiling, a stepper (Submitted → Sales Manager → Finance → Confirmed), and a full audit history.

Routing level comes from the blended risk score against the escalation table: within-limit needs no approval, medium risk needs Sales Manager, high risk needs Sales Manager then Finance. Every approve / return / reject is logged with actor, timestamp, and reason.

3 · Customer negotiation

An approved quote appears in the customer's portal. They can comment per line, propose a counter-discount and delivery date, then submit or confirm outright.

Rule: if the final negotiated terms still fit the approved thresholds, confirming moves straight to confirmed. If they exceed the threshold again, the quote automatically re-enters approval rather than confirming.

4 · Fulfillment

Confirmed orders appear against live per-warehouse stock. The system suggests a warehouse split when one warehouse can't cover the order; ops accepts it or overrides manually. A restock at a short warehouse auto-prompts consolidating the remaining backorder.

5 · Subscriptions & billing

Recurring lines on the original order (Care Plan, Support SLA) spin off a subscription that runs independently of the one-time order's fulfillment. Billing Detail shows both the originating one-time lines and the ongoing recurring lines.

6 · Invoicing & payment

Shipments and billing cycles each generate an invoice. Partial deliveries produce partial invoices, reconciled against what actually shipped rather than the full order. Recording a payment closes the loop.

Frontend implementation notes

  • List vs. detail payloads — keep list endpoints thin (only the columns actually rendered); never return lines[] / history[] on list calls, fetch detail on row click.
  • Shared status vocabulary — one enum per domain server-side (quotations, approvals, fulfillment, invoices); map to badge colors on the frontend, never infer status from other fields.
  • Every button-triggered mutation — submit, approve, return, reject, accept-split, override, modify, cancel, negotiate, confirm, record-payment, send-reminder, escalate, nudge — returns the updated parent resource (or at least its new status) so the UI updates in place without a full refetch.
  • Audit logging — approval actions and discount-config saves persist actor + timestamp + reason server-side regardless of client input, per the Screen 18 compliance requirement.
